Why "paste and hope" fails

The standard move is to drop your resume into an AI chat, ask for a rewrite, and call it done. The problem: a generic rewrite polishes the surface while leaving the real blockers in place. Before a human ever reads your resume, it has to clear two filters that most AI rewrites ignore entirely.

First, an Applicant Tracking System scans for formatting and keyword density. A beautifully written bullet in the wrong place gets scored as a gap. Second, a recruiter spending eight seconds on the page is looking for the exact language from the job post, not your preferred phrasing.

The actual problem

A one-shot AI rewrite fixes your words. It does not audit your ATS score, benchmark your keywords against real job posts, or prepare you for the specific questions a hiring manager will ask based on what you just wrote. That requires a sequence, not a single prompt.

The 4-skill loop 🔁

Each skill hands its output directly to the next. Your resume gets stronger at every checkpoint. This is the same sequence used by applicants targeting Meta, Google, and similarly competitive roles.

Diagnose → Score → Rewrite → Prep
Skill 1
The Diagnoser
Scans your resume exactly as an ATS would. Flags the specific sections that trip up automated screening and tells you precisely how to fix each one.
Skill 2
The Recruiter
Benchmarks your resume against 1,000 similar job posts. Surfaces the keywords you are missing and ranks them by frequency so you know what to prioritize.
Skill 3
The Rewriter
Rebuilds every bullet using Google's XYZ formula: Accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z. Quantified impact, not vague responsibilities.
Skill 4
The Hiring Manager
Runs a live mock interview based on the rewritten resume and scores every answer out of 10, with specific feedback on what to sharpen.

How to run the loop in Claude

Open a single Claude conversation and work through all four skills in sequence without closing the thread. Each skill inherits the context of the previous one, which is what makes the handoff work.

  1. Paste your resume in full at the top of the conversation. Add a note: "This is my current resume. Do not rewrite anything yet." Setting that boundary prevents Claude from jumping ahead before the diagnosis is done.
  2. Run Skill 1: The Diagnoser. Use the prompt below. Read the output carefully before moving on. Fix the flagged issues in your actual document, then paste the updated version back into the same thread.
  3. Run Skill 2: The Recruiter. Paste the job description you are targeting alongside the prompt. Claude will benchmark your resume against that role and surface missing keywords with context.
  4. Run Skill 3: The Rewriter. Claude now has your ATS-passing resume and the keyword list from Skill 2. The rewriter applies the XYZ formula to every bullet using both inputs at once.
  5. Run Skill 4: The Hiring Manager. Ask Claude to take on the role of the hiring manager for the specific company and role. It will ask you real interview questions drawn from your rewritten resume, then score and explain each answer.
  6. Export the final resume and your interview notes. Copy the rewritten resume to your document editor and save the mock interview scoring as a prep sheet before your actual interview.

The prompts, ready to copy

Skill 1 · The Diagnoser
Act as an expert ATS auditor. Scan the resume I have pasted above exactly as a real Applicant Tracking System would.

For each section:
- Identify any formatting, keyword, or structure issue that would cause an ATS to score it lower
- Explain exactly why it is a problem
- Give a specific, actionable fix

Do not rewrite any bullets yet. Diagnose only. Prioritize the top 5 issues by impact.
Skill 2 · The Recruiter
Act as a senior recruiter who has reviewed 1,000 job posts for this type of role.

Here is the job description I am targeting:
[PASTE JOB DESCRIPTION HERE]

Compare my resume (pasted above, now updated) against this role.
- List the keywords and phrases from the job post that are absent or underrepresented in my resume
- Rank them by how frequently they appear across similar job posts
- Note which ones are likely hard filters vs. nice-to-haves
Skill 3 · The Rewriter
Act as a professional resume writer who specializes in tech and business roles at top-tier companies.

Using:
1. The ATS fixes from the Diagnoser above
2. The keyword priorities from the Recruiter above
3. Google's XYZ formula: "Accomplished [X], measured by [Y], by doing [Z]"

Rewrite every bullet point in my experience section.
- Lead with a strong action verb
- Include a quantified result wherever the original implies one
- Weave in the high-priority missing keywords naturally
- Keep each bullet to one or two lines

Show the before and after for each bullet.
Skill 4 · The Hiring Manager
Act as the hiring manager for [COMPANY NAME] reviewing a candidate for [ROLE TITLE].

You have just read the rewritten resume above.

Run a mock interview: ask me 5 questions you would genuinely ask this candidate, one at a time.
After each answer I give, score it out of 10 and explain:
- What worked
- What was missing
- A stronger version of the answer

Begin with your first question now.

What each skill is actually doing 🔍

DiagnoserSimulates how an ATS parses your document: section headers, date formats, table usage, and keyword frequency. Most resumes fail on formatting before a human ever reads a word.
RecruiterMatches your language against the vocabulary of the job market, not just one post. A keyword that appears in 80% of similar posts is a near-certain filter. You need it in your resume.
RewriterGoogle's XYZ formula (Accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z) forces specificity. "Led a team" becomes "Led a team of 6 engineers, reducing deployment time by 40%, by introducing trunk-based development." Same fact, very different signal.
Hiring ManagerBuilds questions from your actual rewritten bullets, so you are preparing for the conversation your resume will prompt, not a generic list. The scoring gives you a baseline before the real thing.

The honest bit ✅

  • The loop works best inside one conversation. If you close the thread and start fresh for each skill, Claude loses the context that makes each handoff useful. Keep it all in one session.
  • Claude cannot access real-time job market data. The Recruiter skill benchmarks based on its training data, not a live scrape of job boards. Cross-check high-priority keywords against two or three actual job posts yourself.
  • The XYZ formula requires real numbers. If your resume has no quantified results, the Rewriter will flag gaps or estimate. You still need to supply the actual metrics. Do not let Claude invent figures.
  • Mock interview scoring is a calibration tool, not a guarantee. A 9 out of 10 from Claude does not mean you are ready. It means you have a strong draft answer worth rehearsing out loud.
  • A great resume still needs a great application strategy. The loop gets you past the filters. Getting your resume in front of the right person, through referrals and direct outreach, remains a separate and equally important job.
